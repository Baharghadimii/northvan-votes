About the candidate

The leadership DNV deserves.

I am Graham Alexander—and I am one of you: a person who wants safe communities, manageable roads, responsible spending, and a future our families can afford.

I bring the experience to do something about it: a UBC Electrical Engineering education, real estate development, complex infrastructure delivery, and the worldwide StayRunners technology business.

Through GGIQ Developments, Graham has worked on major development projects including Lynn Creek, Chilliwack Estates, Mount Thurston Drive, and Mount Archibald Place. The Mount Thurston and Mount Archibald hillside development in Chilliwack reflects the kind of vision, persistence, and delivery required to take a challenging site from concept to a finished community. Through StayRunners, he built and operated a worldwide technology business. He has led complex projects, systems, teams, and organizations from plan to delivery.

DNV needs a mayor who can read the plans, challenge the numbers, bring people together, and deliver. Graham's record in engineering, development, and business gives him the practical foundation to manage a municipality of 70,000 residents—not simply discuss its problems. This is a campaign for the highest standard of leadership: experienced, independent, accountable, and ready from day one.

That background is the reason Graham is prepared to deliver real change. Growth must strengthen DNV, not erase the neighbourhoods people chose to call home. Graham will focus new density around the Yaletown North transit hub, backed by private-capital investment in the roads, utilities, public spaces, and services it needs, while protecting the character of established communities including Seymour, Edgemont, Lynn Valley, and Grouse Woods.

A major international property dispute taught Graham what ordinary people face when systems fail to deliver clear answers, fair process, and accountability.

Reported by The Globe and Mail

A Canadian businessman's fight to get his Mexican hotel back.

The Globe and Mail reported on the 2016 Tulum property dispute involving Graham and other hotel operators. The official ICSID case record also lists Graham as a claimant in the subsequent international arbitration.

What the Globe report describes

  • A disputed eviction: The 2016 report says Graham's 24-room Tulum hotel was among properties affected during a contested beachfront land dispute.
  • A legal fight: It reports that Graham and other affected hotel operators pursued court injunctions and sought answers through legal and public channels.
  • Competing claims: The article describes disputed ownership claims and presents the differing positions of the hotel operators and people supporting the eviction.

This is an original summary of the linked Globe and Mail reporting, not a reproduction of the article.

The USNA framework · Gateway to the North

Three pillars.
One mandate.

You do not need another promise from a distant politician. You need somebody who understands the pressure of daily life in DNV and will take responsibility for solving it. I will put the facts on the table, stop waste, move DNV, protect children, and build smarter.

01 · Stop tax waste

Somebody who will stop waste.

Order an immediate forensic audit of the reported $3.86B North Shore Wastewater Plant budget disaster. Publish the contracts, change orders, delays, approvals, and responsible decision points. Where evidence warrants it, refer matters to the RCMP and request international coordination through proper authorities.

The campaign is calling for lawful investigation; it does not claim that any person or organization has been found guilty.

02 · Protect children and families

Somebody who will protect our children.

I will not accept injection or supervised-consumption sites being forced into communities near daycares, schools, playgrounds, or child-centred spaces. Families should not be told that exposure to the risks of fentanyl and heroin is simply the price of living in their neighbourhood.

I will demand meaningful local consultation, safe siting, strong security, treatment pathways, and public accountability. Compassion and public safety must work together—and children must come first.

03 · Preserve communities and build smarter

Somebody who will protect home—and plan growth properly.

I will preserve the character of established neighbourhoods—including Edgemont, Lynn Valley, and Grouse Woods—by directing new density to Yaletown North around the transit hub. That growth must be matched by massive, upfront investment in roads, transit connections, utilities, public spaces, and community services so the area can flourish.

I will also fix bridge and arterial gridlock with modern traffic synchronization, federal USNA backing, and housing where people work. Support new rental construction connected to major employers such as Seaspan so workers can live closer to jobs instead of adding daily cross-North Shore congestion.

I will deploy AI-powered instant building permits to cut municipal review delays from months to minutes—while keeping accountable human oversight.

I am somebody who will do for the people.

Residents deserve public timelines, responsible officials, project costs, safety standards, housing targets, and progress reports—not excuses. I will put every major commitment on the record and report back to the people.

Love in action · A compassionate DNV

Solving North Vancouver's
homelessness crisis—with compassion.

Every mayor must lead with compassion for people experiencing homelessness. DNV can show the world what love, care, safety, and a real path forward look like.

Graham believes street-level crises require a better response: dignified support for people in need and safer public spaces for families, schools, daycares, and neighbourhoods. His campaign proposal is a dedicated, phased recovery facility away from residential centres, in the area behind Capilano University, subject to feasibility, public consultation, and required approvals.

Watch the homelessness recovery video ↗ Read the recovery plan

1 · Secure stabilization

Safety, food, shelter, and dignity.

Meet immediate needs with safe shelter, nutritious food, and stable care for people who need support before taking their next step.

2 · Healing and recovery

Mental-health and rehabilitation support.

Connect people with counselling, recovery supports, and practical care that rebuilds confidence and prepares them for independent living.

3 · A path to independence

Transitional homes and work opportunities.

Create peaceful transitional housing with essential services and meaningful work opportunities so residents can earn, contribute, and move forward.

The goal is simple: compassionate support for people in need, and cleaner, safer neighbourhoods for every DNV family. That is love in action.
