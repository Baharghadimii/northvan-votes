Traffic and transportation

Traffic and transportation in the District of North Vancouver

Getting across the District should not be the hardest part of the day. The District controls its own roads, signals, sidewalks and construction permits; the Province owns Highway 1 and both bridges; TransLink runs the buses.

This page says which is which, and what a mayor can actually move.

What a mayor can do

  • Chair the council that adopts the District’s Transportation Plan and its annual capital budget for roads, crossings and cycling connections.
  • Sit on the TransLink Mayors’ Council, where every mayor in the region has a seat and a vote on investment plans and service levels.
  • Direct, through council, how the District coordinates and communicates its own construction.
  • Speak for the District to the Province on highway and bridge congestion — persistently, with the numbers in hand, and without pretending the District controls them.

What Max proposes

Improve Traffic, Park Parking and Construction Coordination: the proposals

A public list of District bottlenecks within 100 days, better coordination to reduce avoidable overlapping closures, and simpler resident parking at parks. Each proposal says how it would be done, who has to agree, and what it costs or gives up.

1Improve coordination of road construction Proposal

One published District construction calendar, and a working rule that two neighbouring corridors are not closed at the same time when the District controls the permit.

Who has to agree
District engineering staff; utilities; the Province for highway work.
The trade-off
Sequencing projects can make some of them take longer. The calendar says so in advance instead of surprising people.

2Advocate for more reliable public transportation Proposal

Use the Mayors’ Council seat to press for North Shore bus frequency and for bus rapid transit, with a single North Shore position agreed with the City and West Vancouver.

Who has to agree
TransLink; the Mayors’ Council; the other two North Shore mayors.
The trade-off
TransLink decides. A mayor’s tools are persistence and a united North Shore, not a promise of a route.

3Improve traffic flow Proposal

Signal-timing reviews on the District’s own arterials, published with before-and-after results.

Who has to agree
District engineering staff.
The trade-off
Timing that favours through traffic can lengthen pedestrian waits at crossings. The results are published so residents can judge the balance.

4Make walking and cycling safer Proposal

Keep delivering the active-transportation projects already in the Transportation Plan, starting with school routes and town centres.

Who has to agree
District staff; the Province’s cost-share programs for cycling infrastructure.
The trade-off
Protected space for walking and cycling comes out of road width or parking. Each project says which.

5Recognize the needs of drivers, seniors and people with disabilities Proposal

Accessible crossings and bus stops in every town-centre project, and HandyDART reliability raised at the Mayors’ Council.

Who has to agree
District staff; TransLink.
The trade-off
Accessibility work is unglamorous and competes with bigger projects for the same budget.

6Work with regional and provincial transportation partners Proposal

A standing North Shore transportation table with the City, West Vancouver and the Province, with its notes published.

Who has to agree
City of North Vancouver; District of West Vancouver; Province of BC; TransLink.
The trade-off
Tables produce agreement slowly. Publishing the notes is what keeps them honest.

Your questions

What is Max Zahedi’s plan for traffic in North Vancouver?

Coordinate the District’s own road construction so neighbouring corridors are not closed at once, publish the schedule, review signal timing on District arterials, keep building safer walking and cycling routes, and use the mayor’s seat on the TransLink Mayors’ Council to push for more reliable North Shore transit. He does not promise to fix bridge or highway congestion, which the Province controls.

Can the Mayor of the District of North Vancouver fix the Lions Gate or Ironworkers bridges?

No. Both bridges and Highway 1 belong to the Province of British Columbia. A mayor can make the case to the Province and to TransLink, ideally alongside the other North Shore mayors, but cannot widen, re-time or rebuild a provincial crossing.

Who is responsible for Lynn Valley Road and Mountain Highway?

The District of North Vancouver. They are District roads, so their signals, crossings, lane design and construction scheduling are decisions the District makes and a mayor and council can change.
