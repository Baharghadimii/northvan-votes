About Graham, in order

Six steps that prepared Graham to lead DNV.

This is not a list of titles. It is a straightforward record of learning, building, and leading—from his DNV beginnings to the experience he now brings to public service.

01 · DNV beginnings

Raised in Lynn Valley. Educated at Argyle.

Graham grew up in Lynn Valley and attended Argyle Secondary School. DNV is home, which is why protecting its neighbourhoods, families, small businesses, and natural setting matters personally to him.

02 · Engineering

Electrical Engineering at UBC.

Graham went on to earn his Electrical Engineering education at UBC. It shaped his practical approach to infrastructure: examine the evidence, test assumptions, control costs, and deliver.

03 · Finance

Investment banking at Canaccord Capital.

At Canaccord Capital in Vancouver, Graham gained investment-banking experience that informs his focus on due diligence, responsible investment, transparent reporting, and making every tax dollar work harder.

04 · Development

Building a new hillside community in Chilliwack.

Through GGIQ Developments, Graham helped build the Mount Thurston Drive and Mount Archibald Place estates in Chilliwack, alongside work on Lynn Creek and Chilliwack Estates. It is the kind of coordinated development work that requires land, infrastructure, construction, financing, and community planning to work together.

05 · Hospitality and entrepreneurship

Building businesses from Tulum to the world.

Graham's work in hospitality included a Tulum beach hotel. He later founded and operated the worldwide StayRunners business, building technology-enabled systems and leading teams and partners across markets.

06 · Leadership and partnership

A business partner of Kyle R. Washington.

Graham is a business partner of Kyle R. Washington, Executive Chairman of Seaspan. The relationship reflects Graham's experience working with accomplished business leaders and his readiness to bring that perspective to DNV.

From Lynn Valley to UBC, Canaccord Capital, real estate development, hospitality, global entrepreneurship, and major-business leadership: Graham has built the practical credentials to lead DNV through change.

Graham's business relationship with Kyle R. Washington does not imply an endorsement by Mr. Washington, Seaspan, or The Washington Companies.

The mayoral agenda, point by point

A Mayor's plan for the DNV residents deserve.

Experience only matters when it produces better outcomes. Graham's platform focuses that experience on the everyday issues residents raise most often—and on a District Hall that answers to the people it serves.

01 · Accountable spending

Put the facts on the table.

Pursue a forensic audit of the reported North Shore Wastewater Treatment Plant cost escalation, publish major project information, and give residents clear progress reports.

02 · Protect neighbourhoods

Build smarter, not everywhere.

Direct growth to the Yaletown North transit hub with the roads, utilities, public spaces, and services it needs—while protecting the character of Seymour, Edgemont, Lynn Valley, Grouse Woods, and other established communities.

03 · Fix gridlock

Move people and goods reliably.

Advance practical traffic synchronization, transit connections, housing closer to major employers, and infrastructure planning that relieves pressure on North Shore roads.

04 · Safer communities

Compassion and safety together.

Support a recovery-focused response to homelessness with shelter, food, treatment, transitional housing, essential services, and pathways to work—while keeping family spaces and neighbourhoods safe.

05 · Faster public service

Modernize how District Hall works.

Use accountable technology to make building permits faster, improve access to public information, and ensure residents can see who owns a local issue and what happens next.

06 · A direct line to residents

Make City Hall answerable.

Create a district-wide resident platform so neighbours can raise concerns, follow municipal responses, find meeting information, and communicate directly with the Mayor's Office.

Yaletown North · Lynn Creek

A modern-day Alfred Taylor vision.

Graham's vision draws inspiration from Alfred Taylor and the partnership that helped bring the Lions Gate Bridge to life with the Guinness family. He proposes a Yaletown North Bridge dedicated to local traffic and a revitalized Lynn Creek: Yaletown North, a gateway to the North built for the modern-day gold rush.

Through the USNA framework, Graham sets out an Arctic-to-Yucatan vision that links DNV's future growth to local infrastructure, local opportunity, and a stronger gateway to the North.

StayRunners worldwide

Founder and businessman with global operating experience.

Graham has run StayRunners, a worldwide business, giving him direct experience operating across markets, building technology-enabled systems, managing teams and partners, and turning an ambitious idea into an operating organization. He brings an entrepreneur's focus on execution, accountability, and making every dollar work harder.

Standing up for property rights

Taking a dispute to an international forum.

Graham was a Canadian claimant in Carlos Sastre and others v. United Mexican States (ICSID Case No. UNCT/20/2), an arbitration administered by the World Bank Group's International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes. The tribunal issued its award in 2022.

That experience reflects the resolve Graham brings to public life: when the stakes are high, he does the work, stands up for what he believes is right, and sees difficult matters through.

Direct communication with residents

An open line to the Mayor's Office.

Graham will introduce a district-wide, Nextdoor-style resident platform so neighbours can raise local issues, share information, follow municipal responses, and communicate directly with the Mayor's Office. The goal is simple: residents should be able to see that their concern was received, know who is responsible, and follow what happens next.

This is a commitment to accessible leadership, not closed-door politics. Graham will make resident communication, meeting information, decisions, and progress reporting easier to find and harder to ignore. District Hall must answer to the people it serves.

Compassion and recovery

A practical response to homelessness.

Graham's homelessness-recovery proposal is grounded in dignity, care, recovery, and independence. It calls for safe shelter and food, mental-health and rehabilitation supports, transitional housing, essential services, and pathways to work.

The campaign proposes a dedicated, phased recovery facility away from residential centres, subject to feasibility, public consultation, and required approvals—supporting people in crisis while helping keep public spaces safe for families, schools, daycares, and neighbourhoods.
