Privacy policy

Last updated: September 26, 2026

What is collected

Only what you type into a form on this site. That may include your name, email address, telephone number, postal code, neighbourhood, address (for lawn sign requests), languages spoken, availability, the issues you care about and your message.

The site does not ask for, and must never be used to collect, financial account details, government identification numbers or passwords.

Why it is collected

  • To reply to you.
  • To organise volunteering, lawn signs and events.
  • To send campaign updates, if you asked for them.
  • To understand which issues matter in which neighbourhoods.
  • To meet record-keeping obligations under BC election law, where those apply.

How it is shared

Your information is used by the campaign team only. It is not sold, rented or traded. It is shared outside the campaign only where the law requires it — most relevantly, Elections BC requires campaigns to report contributions of $100 or more, including the contributor's name.

How long it is kept

Campaign records are kept for as long as BC election law requires and are then deleted. If you ask to be removed from campaign communications before then, that request is honoured immediately.

Your choices

  • Unsubscribe — every campaign email carries a working unsubscribe link, and you can also email the campaign directly.
  • Access — ask what the campaign holds about you and it will be provided.
  • Correction and deletion — ask for a correction or removal and it will be actioned, except where election law requires a record to be kept.

Cookies and analytics

This site sets no advertising or tracking cookies, and carries no third-party analytics, advertising pixels or social media trackers. Opening a page contacts no company other than the one hosting it — the typefaces are served from this domain rather than from Google Fonts — with one exception: the map of voting places on the voting page, which is served by Google and loads as you scroll down to it. The Instagram posts at the top of the homepage are not an exception: this site’s own server fetches them from Instagram and serves their pictures itself, so your browser contacts Instagram only if you tap a post or the “Follow on Instagram” link. Your browser may store small preferences locally — a chosen language, whether you picked the light or dark theme, or an unsent form draft — which stays on your device and is never sent to the campaign. The only cookie this site ever sets is for the campaign’s own administrator while logged in; visitors get none.

Visit counts

The campaign counts visits to its own pages, on its own server, to see which pages people read and roughly where interest is coming from. Each page view sends one small message to this website containing the page, the site that linked to it (if any), your browser’s language setting, the width of your screen (to tell a phone from a desktop) and, on the voting page, which language you chose. No cookie is set and no outside service is involved. Your network address is used for two things and then discarded: to look up an approximate location — usually a city — in a database held on the campaign’s own server, and to make a scrambled daily identifier that lets the campaign count how many different people visited on a given day. That identifier changes every day and cannot be turned back into an address, so nobody can be followed from one day to the next, and the address itself is never stored. If your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal, nothing is sent at all. Counts are kept for no more than 13 months.

The campaign’s videos, the short one on the homepage, the eight-minute one and the short ones on the Farsi, Chinese and Tagalog pages, are served from this website, not from YouTube or any other company. If you play one, the same kind of message records that it was played, on which page, how many seconds of it you watched, the furthest point you reached and whether it reached the end, with the same scrambled daily identifier and the width of your screen. To keep those as one record, the page makes up a random code for that single page view; the code is not stored on your device and is gone when you leave the page. Nothing is recorded if you do not press play, or if your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal.

The map of voting places

The voting page shows the District of North Vancouver’s map of voting places, which is served by Google Maps. It is not requested when the page opens; your browser loads it as you scroll down to the “Where to vote” section — at which point Google receives the request and may set its own cookies, as it would on any site carrying a Google map. The District’s own website, linked beneath the map, carries the same map.

Finding your nearest voting place

The voting page, and the Chinese, Farsi and Tagalog pages, can show the voting places nearest you. It works on your own device: the page downloads one list of North Vancouver postal codes and their approximate positions from this website, and your browser compares your postal code with it. Your postal code is not sent to the campaign or to anyone else, and nothing is kept. If you press “Use my location”, your browser asks your permission first and then tells the page where you are; that stays on your device too, although your browser may use its own location service to work it out. The “Directions” links open Google Maps, which then receives the address of the voting place you chose, and nothing else from this site.

Reminders and campaign messages

If you sign up for voting reminders or campaign updates, your name, email address, mobile number (if you gave one) and area are stored in a database on the campaign’s own server, outside the public web folder. No email-marketing company holds the list. The campaign records the date and time you agreed, the address the form was sent from, and the exact sentence you ticked, because Canada’s anti-spam law asks for that record. Emails are sent from this domain; every one carries a working unsubscribe link and the headers that let your mail program unsubscribe you in one click. Text messages are sent only to people who ticked the separate text-message box, through Twilio, a messaging carrier that receives your number and the message and nothing else; reply STOP to any text to end them. Unsubscribing closes that channel immediately. The record itself is kept for as long as BC election law requires and is then deleted.

Have your say (the poll)

The poll questions are anonymous. An answer is stored on the campaign’s own server with the question, the option chosen, the area you gave if you gave one, and the same scrambled daily identifier the visit counter uses, so that one person counts once per question per day. No name, email or address is stored with an answer, and nothing joins an answer to the contact list. Your browser remembers which questions you have answered so it can show you the results; “Change my answer” forgets that and your next answer replaces the earlier one for the day. Results for a question are shown to anyone who has answered it, with the number of answers, and the two questions marked “for the campaign only” are never shown to anyone outside the campaign.

Offline storage

If your connection drops while you are filling in a form, your answers are kept in your own browser so they are not lost. They stay on your device until you send them or clear your browser data.

Accessibility statement

This site is built to meet WCAG 2.2 Level AA. Accessibility is not a feature of a campaign website; a voter who cannot read the voting information is a voter who was shut out.

What has been done

  • Full keyboard navigation, with a visible focus indicator on every interactive element.
  • A skip-to-content link at the top of every page.
  • Colour contrast checked against AA thresholds for body text, headings and buttons.
  • Text that stays usable when enlarged to 200 per cent.
  • Form fields with real labels, clear required-field marking, and error messages that name the problem.
  • Status messages announced to screen readers.
  • Descriptive alternative text on images, and captions on published photographs.
  • A logical heading structure on every page.
  • Animation reduced automatically when your device asks for reduced motion.
  • A light and a dark theme, following your device by default and switchable from any page. Both were checked against the AA contrast thresholds, not just the light one.
  • Pages that remain readable and printable without JavaScript.

Known limitations

  • Translations of the voting essentials are pending review by native speakers.
  • The eight-minute video has English captions and Farsi subtitles, and the record page explains every graph in it in text.
  • The short videos on the Farsi, Chinese and Tagalog pages print every spoken sentence on screen, but have no separate transcript yet.

Tell us

If any part of this site prevents you from getting the information you need, contact the campaign and it will be fixed. If you need campaign information in another format — large print, plain text, read aloud over the phone — ask and it will be provided.

Campaign contribution rules

Contributions to BC municipal campaigns are governed by the Local Elections Campaign Financing Act and administered by Elections BC.

  • Only individuals may contribute. Organizations, corporations, unions and associations may not.
  • Contributors must be residents of British Columbia and Canadian citizens or permanent residents.
  • The 2026 individual limit is $1,429.70 per campaign.
  • Contributions of $100 or more are publicly reported, including the contributor's name.
  • You must contribute your own money. Being reimbursed by someone else, or contributing on another person's behalf, is prohibited.
  • Municipal campaign contributions do not receive tax receipts.

Contributions are accepted by Interac e-Transfer to donate@maxformayor.ca, into the campaign account controlled by the financial agent, who records the contributor's name and address. Card payments are not offered on this site.
