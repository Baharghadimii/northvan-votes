Meet Max

North Vancouver has been my home for 38 years.

This is where I built my life, raised my family and became part of a community I deeply care about. Through my work in technology, public service, business and community organizations, I have learned the value of listening, working with others and following through.

I am running for mayor because I know this community, understand the pressures residents are facing and believe I have the experience to improve how the District works for them.

Voting day

  1. Voting day Saturday, October 17, 2026

    Residents decide

    • Residents choose a mayor and council for the next four years. If Max is elected, the walk starts here. How and where to vote →

  2. Day 1 The first regular Council meeting

    Two motions on the first day

    • A motion to launch the public commitment tracker. Read the priority →
    • A motion to launch the North Shore wastewater fairness campaign, with a petition open to residents, businesses and neighbouring councils. Read the priority →

  3. Day 100 Within the first 100 days

    Every promise tracked in public

    • A personal commitment tracker published, showing deadlines, Council decisions, progress and evidence, and Council support sought for an official delivery dashboard. Read the priority →
    • Council support sought to publish a ranking of the ten worst District traffic bottlenecks. Read the priority →

  4. Month 6 Within six months

    A costed plan for every commitment

    • An implementation plan covering every priority and neighbourhood commitment: the staff time and spending each needs, first-year actions against later-term projects, and what depends on funding or a Council decision. Read the priority →

  5. Year 1 The first year

    The groundwork year

    A line-by-line budget, published plainly. A public account of the wastewater plant’s cost, with independent oversight pressed for at Metro Vancouver. One road-construction calendar. Service standards for municipal hall.

    • A core service review proposed, to find savings, duplication and services that could be delivered better. Read the priority →
    • A feasibility and financing plan for the 1,000 affordable rental and co-op homes, naming sites, capacity, affordability levels and milestones. Read the priority →

  6. Years 2–3 Years two and three

    Growth with the services to match

    Homes in the town centres with the roads, pipes, schools and parks to match. Wildfire and emergency plans reported to residents, not just filed. One North Shore voice on transit at the Mayors’ Council.

The next few years

Strong North Vancouver, United Canada

Canada faces tariff pressure and trade disputes with the United States. Trade policy is Ottawa’s; running the District carefully through hard times is the mayor’s. Here is what that means.

Relief that already exists, and what the District can add:Groceries and foodRentersWorkersSmall businessAll 9 sectors →

What a mayor cannot do

I will not promise what a mayor cannot deliver. Under section 25 of the Community Charter, the District cannot lower grocery prices, cut one sector's taxes or fees, or hand money to a business. What it can do is run its own house well, help the organisations that already feed and house our neighbours, and make sure every resident knows about the help that already exists.

Confirmed fact Community Charter, section 25

This is not a promise that the next few years will be easy. It is a promise about how the District will be run while they are hard.
