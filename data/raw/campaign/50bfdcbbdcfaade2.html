Core Values

What you can expect from me.

Most campaigns lead with promises. I want to lead with a standard, because the promises only mean something if you know how I'll actually behave once the cameras are off and the hard votes come up.

Good governance isn't complicated. Show up. Have a plan. Be honest. Do what you said you'd do. That's the job. It's easy to say and harder to live up to, which is exactly why it's worth putting in writing before you've given me your vote rather than after.

This page is about the kind of councillor I intend to be. It is not a list of promises. Promises are specific, they can be checked, and they belong on the Commitments page where you can hold me to them.

What follows is the part that comes before that. How I will show up, how I will decide, and what I will tell you when I get something wrong.

01Show up

Residents deserve leadership that listens and is present, not just in the weeks before an election. Too many decisions get made without most people ever hearing about them until the outcome is already set.

I'll be at the table, in the community, and reachable in between. That includes making a real effort to reach the people councils usually talk past: renters, younger families, and residents like me who will live longest with the choices this council makes. If a decision is going to shape the District for thirty years, the people who'll be here in thirty years deserve a say in it.

02Have a plan

Good intentions don't fix aging infrastructure, and they don't bring down the cost of living here. The work is in the details, and the details take homework.

I come to the table prepared. I read the budgets, I dug into the files, and I will bring forward ideas that hold up to scrutiny rather than ones that just sound good in a headline. I'd rather show up with a workable idea and get told no than show up with nothing. And I'll be honest about what one councillor among seven can and can't deliver, because a plan that oversells itself isn't a plan, it's a pitch.

03Be honest

Trust starts with clear communication and open budgets, even when the news is hard. You can't trust a council that only tells you the easy half of the story.

That means plain language instead of process jargon, real numbers instead of reassurance, and a straight answer when the answer is difficult. When I don't know something, I'll say so. When I get something wrong, I'll own it. The goal isn't to look right, it's to give residents an accurate picture of where things actually stand so you can hold your council to it.

04Do what I said I'd do

Accountability isn't a slogan. It only counts if you can check it.

So I'll publish my voting record and keep it public, so you can measure my choices against the commitments I made to earn your vote. If I said I'd push for something and then voted the other way, you should be able to see that plainly and ask me why. That's the one promise I can keep entirely on my own, without needing four other votes to get there, and I intend to keep it.

A note on transparency. This page has evolved as the campaign has grown. If you'd like to see where it started, the earlier version of my core values is still public here. Nothing disappears, nothing gets quietly edited.
