Platform

On this page: Housing & cost of living  ·  Active transportation  ·  Taxes & spending  ·  Parks & climate  ·  Emergency preparedness  ·  Childcare, seniors, homelessness & community services  ·  Schools  ·  Local economy  ·  Animals & bears

Building Community for All

Strengthening a community where everyone feels welcomed, included, connected, and supported. Working toward a community where people of different ages, incomes, and stages of life can afford to live, work, and build their futures.

A Commitment to Residents – Governance and Transparency

Every voice deserves to be heard. I believe local government works best when it is accountable, works collaboratively, makes responsible decisions, and is focused on the needs of all the people it serves, especially the most vulnerable.

Priorities – My top 2 – Affordable Housing & Cost of Living and Active Transportation

Development and Growth – Affordable Housing and Cost of Living

We need diverse housing options – advocating for sensible development that increases housing supply, including options for young families buying their first home, rental options and accessible housing for seniors wanting to age in place.

We need to support housing developments near transit and key hubs so local workers, teachers, first responders and healthcare staff can afford to live in the community where they work.

Ensuring new housing developments are paired with necessary community infrastructure, public transit, and school planning to maintain neighborhood livability.

A really good example is the new Kiwanis development in West Vancouver where they have allocated spaces for school staff, first responders and healthcare employees.

Options to review and implement are:

  • Mid-market rentals — for low-and moderate-income earners who don't earn enough to afford today's market rents
  • Family-friendly rentals (3+ bedrooms) for renting families who need more space
  • Senior-oriented housing — for seniors looking to stay in the community as their needs change
  • Rent-to-own options — for first-time buyers who can't assemble a down payment
  • Flexible and multi-generational forms — townhouses, cohousing, duplexes, and homes with secondary suites or coach houses that can serve as a mortgage helper or let family members age in place

Active Transportation

Supporting safe, accessible, and practical transportation options that make it easier for people to move throughout the District. We need safer streets and intersections, more sidewalks and marked crosswalks, improved lighting and consistent rules for e-bikes and e-scooters as that has become a growing issue.

As our community grows, I am committed to advocating for safe, accessible and practical active transportation options for all North Vancouver residents – whether you’re walking, cycling or driving. There needs to be a safe walking and cycling network that spans the whole North Shore. There needs to be more safe protected bike lanes and walkable school zones so more kids are active, healthier and ready to learn when they arrive at school.

I would advocate for more frequent buses to make it a better option for residents. There is not sufficient or timely service on many routes in the District of North Vancouver. There needs to be more effort put in to make that happen.

I would advocate and work with TransLink for FREE transit for ages 13 to 18 so there is less congestion at high schools and more independence for youth to get around safely by bus. This has been implemented by the Manitoba Premier – Wab Kinew just recently. Also, Safe and Active Routes to School Program – we need to see what infrastructure, signage and updates can be made so it is safe for kids to walk and bike to school to decrease the amount of cars at pickup and drop off times – too much congestion and safety concerns.

Also, I would advocate for the runaway lanes to be put in that were originally planned when the highway upgrade was built. They were designed to help North Shore residents not to get stuck in the traffic congestion but be able to bypass all that and get from one end of the North Shore to the other end in a timely manner.

Property Taxes and Spending – Wastewater Treatment Plant

Finding other avenues for funding instead of raising taxes. The amount of taxes has grown quite substantially over the years and the services offered to residents in return are not visible.

There is already a huge burden on North Vancouver Residents with the Wastewater Treatment Plant costs for the next 30 years with no actual explanation or advocacy plans shared out. The wastewater plant went from $300M to $700M to $1B and now $4B – which doesn’t include the removal of the old wastewater plant and remedial efforts there

Why did it take 7 years for the residents on the North Shore to know about the $4B cost. We have reps from each municipality there – why were no yearly updates provided? If the whistleblower didn’t leak the information, when were the NV Reps going to speak up! Residents need to ask more questions and get concrete answers and have their elected council advocate for them.

There are many grants that are available to municipalities to apply for provincially and federally that are options for DNV. We can also partner with CNV and DWV and do joint collaborative projects and pool resources.

There needs to be a fresh overview of all the funds collected and how they are allocated out. With a new CAO and new council – it is good governance and operationally sound to do this and put in a new strategy for the next 4 years.

Parks & Green Spaces, Environment Stewardship and Climate Action

Active transportation and safe infrastructure – expanding connected pathways, safe bike lanes and walkable school zones to reduce local traffic congestion and vehicle emissions

Protected parks and green spaces – preserving North Vancouver’s beautiful natural parks, urban tree canopy and sensitive ecosystems while ensuring accessible outdoor spaces for all families. Have more residents get involved in community cleanups, weeds clearing and planting and creating more gardens – increase connectivity, food and sustainability.

Sustainable district operations – supporting energy-efficient municipal buildings, climate resilient infrastructure planning and waste reduction initiatives in partnership with local schools and community organizations. More education on what is garbage, what should be recycled, how to use food scraps and leaves for compost in your home gardens. Create info sheets and workshop events for residents and share in schools.

Community collaboration – working alongside local environmental groups, Squamish Nation and Tsleil-Waututh Nation leadership and youth climate advocates to build collaborative community-led sustainability efforts.

I have taken part in the North Vancouver School District Sustainability Committee and Climate Action Workgroups across BC and Canada. I worked on the “Master of Disaster” program for Grade 5 Students across BC with Prepared BC.

I have supported teachers and students with school garden and climate action initiatives and helped get them grant funding from organizations. I have taken part in school and ocean cleanups led by students. I share and promote the DNV provides funding through their Sustainability Grant Program for schools and community organizations

I have advocated for active transportation and safe infrastructure around schools to have more connected pathways, safe bike lanes and walkable school zones to reduce local traffic congestion and vehicle emissions.

Advocate for more transit – reliable and more buses – make it a more reliable and cost effective choice to residents instead of them driving everywhere. It would reduce so much congestion on our roads.

Rebate incentives can be provided to residents to reduce their carbon footprint.

People need to be mindful of waste, how much energy they are using, water consumption at home, lawn watering in the summer, be more proactive with their usage.

Students are doing great things with their environmental groups – I would continue to support and encourage them with their efforts. Also help them find funding options for their initiatives.

There should be more focus on care for your neighborhood & community – less littering, cigarette butts, garbage, etc …. keeping it clean and a beautiful home for everyone.

Emergency Preparedness

I would work closely with the DNV Staff to find out what is already in place and what more can be done to protect and put in place to reduce risk and damages in case of an emergency. The North Shore has had a few rainfall, flooding and sea level rise instances already. I would review what were the steps taken.

I would consult with our first responders, NSEM – North Shore Emergency Management and Prepared BC for the North Shore wide safety plan. Also ensure all residents are prepared at home and work and have a plan in case of emergency. The firefighters can come and do an assessment at your home – I would promote that and get more residents to take part.

For flooding – residents need to do their part in cleaning drains near their homes and leaves to keep water flowing and decrease flooding opportunities.

More prep work needs to be done when rainfall warnings are issued so we are prepared beforehand and we can put in place as many safety measures we can for safety of all residents.

For the extreme heat events and colder weather spaces – increase the number of spaces for residents as needed. Currently the libraries and community centers are providing these welcome spaces.

More education has to be provided for the wildfire risks at camp grounds, parks, trails and any forested areas. There have to be consequences/fines for those who do not comply. There are too many human caused fires and the cost rises each year.

  • The Alertable App - residents to have access and download it.
  • DNV Fire – they are doing home safety inspections – connect with them and sign up
  • BC Shakeout – Do you take part in this drill every October 10? Practice being prepared not when the emergency happens.
  • Home/Work Preparedness – do you have a plan for your family/workplace? You should be prepared for 3 days to 2 weeks – emergency kits and a 24 hour grab-and-go bag for each person and pet and 4 litres of water per person daily. Visit www.preparedbc.ca

Childcare, Seniors, Homelessness, and Community Services

Childcare

This is a very high need for families with young children – especially where both parents have to work to pay their bills.

There have been several new childcare spaces added across North Vancouver.

I have advocated and supported families for the past 15 years when I was a PAC Chair, NV DPAC Rep and as a School Board Trustee.

All schools have some form of childcare space on the premises. In the last 4 years there were 4 childcare spaces opened at Dorothy Lynas, Seymour Heights, Montroyal Elementary Schools and a Childcare Centre at Cloverley Elementary School.

I would continue to advocate provincially and federally to support families with the care they needed – before and after care, childcare and other – whatever best suits their needs. Also for capital funding to build more new spaces and work with local providers.

Seniors

There needs to be more options provided for them to downgrade and transition into affordable housing. There needs to be more opportunities available to them to live full lives and access the services and programs on the North Shore.

The new Silver Harbour Seniors Centre is a great new space for them. I would work collaboratively with them and see how I could help and support them.

I would like to see more connections being made as a community to have check ins so there is less loneliness and they feel more supported.

Homelessness

Homelessness can happen to anyone and does – a lot of people are living from paycheque to paycheque. There are a lot of different reasons why and we need to offer support and help and not judgement. There needs to be more opportunities provided for support and assist with transition housing and employment to get them back on their feet.

I would be advocating for more transitional and affordable housing options. One idea I really like is the “Tiny Homes Villages” being built across North America and different versions across the world. They offer supports and a home to help situate and give a sense of a safe home for them.

I would work collaboratively with the North Shore Homelessness Task Force and see what I can do to help support their efforts and advocate.

Community Services

I would collaborate with NVREC and other organizations to share out what they are providing for all ages and share them out – there is not enough communication currently and not a lot of residents know what is available for them to sign up for. There are also lots of FREE and low cost activities that are available for families.

There are many sports organizations seeking support and I would work with them to find solutions to their questions and see how I can help them. I am listening and feel these and other organizations across North Vancouver provide valuable and important opportunities for all our residents – youth to senior.

Schools and Education

I have been highly engaged and connected in my role as an elected NVSD School Board Trustee for the past 8 years. I have built trust and made lasting relationships with Senior Exec, Staff, NVTA – Teachers, CUPE Support Staff, NoVA – Principals & VPs, LSTs, EAs, Custodians and Ministry of Education.

I would continue to advocate for NVSD and education in my new role. Education for our children should matter to everyone and we want them to become educated citizens to give back to our communities and achieve their dreams and be happy.

You can read a more detailed summary in the NVSD School Board Trustee Record.

Local Economy and Small Business

Our local businesses are the heart and soul of the DNV – they build vibrant communities and create local jobs for our residents.

Having served our community for over 25 years, I know how important it is for local government to listen to business owners and create an environment where they can thrive. If elected, I will commit to supporting sensible policies that reduce red tape, improve vibrant commercial hubs and keep our local economy strong.

I would also highlight and support small businesses and be open to listen and work together so they can be successful and provide their services to DNV residents of all ages. I would meet with and support local community associations as well.

Animals and Bears

We need to co-exist and be more responsible with garbage, fruit trees, gardens and other attractants (ex: hummingbird feeders) as homeowners and residents

From a candidate survey on co-existing with bears

Thank you for your email and question: Besides education, what specific mechanisms would you implement in your municipality to better co-exist with bears?

I have been collaborating with North Shore Black Bear Society to share out education materials to students at schools and parents in the community through my work with North Shore Safety Council for over 10 years.

To move beyond education and create urban co-existence with bears, municipalities need to enforce the following: infrastructure upgrades, targeted regulatory penalties and planning solutions.

  • Automated Lock-and-Latch Carts - these are the carts everyone has - but residents need to be diligent that there is no damage and they are latched correctly
  • Enclosed Commercial Enclosures - small businesses, multi family complexes and parks to house the dumpsters in mandatory heavy duty concrete and steel bear resistant enclosures
  • Stricter Curbside Timing and Enforced by Bylaws - for those not following rules to timing of putting out garbage
  • Ban High-Attractant Vegetation - update municipal landscaping guidelines to prohibit planting high risk attractants
  • Create wildlife corridors - conduct a bear hazard assessment and apply zoning laws to steer bears around residential hubs instead of through them
  • Community Fruit Gleaning Network - we already have a lot of volunteers doing this - increase the call out for this and get more people engaged and take part

Education is always important so I would recommend the “Bear Smart Community Program” resources and the key components they have compiled:

Key Components

  • Establish a Bear Smart Committee that Oversees planning and implementation and Includes local government, wildlife agencies, NGOs, and community members.
  • Conduct a Community Assessment — Identifies attractants (garbage, fruit trees, livestock feed) and Review local bear activity and conflict history
  • Develop a Bear Smart Community Plan — Outlines strategies for waste management, education, enforcement, and habitat considerations.
  • Implement the Plan — Install bear-resistant bins, Improve bylaws and enforcement and Conduct public education campaigns.

A brochure outlining the program and a technical background report are available:

  • Be ‘Bear Smart’ Community Brochure (PDF, 7 MB)
  • Bear Smart Community Program: Background Report (PDF, 527 KB)

I think this is a big issue that has grown over the years and we all have to do our part to work collaboratively together and protect the bears and prevent any safety issues that could occur.
