05 · Growth & Neighbourhood Planning

A developer can propose a project. The City and its residents should plan the community.

Growth decisions should show meaningful choices, protect public assets and account for infrastructure over the long term.

5.1

Developer-initiated OCP amendments

Show meaningful alternatives before selecting a preferred direction.

For a material OCP amendment, invite genuinely different applicant concepts before a preferred scheme is fixed. Compare the existing plan, multiple concepts where practical and a City- or community-tested alternative where useful. Planning scenarios are enough if they clearly show the tradeoffs.

Use the full process for material amendments, not housekeeping changes.

5.2

First Street Municipal Park

This looks like a park because people already use it as one. Let’s make it permanent.

Reverse the previous Council’s residential designation and zoning for the City-owned East First lands between Lonsdale and St. Georges. Amend the OCP and zoning to make these lands a permanent municipal park, with substantial underground parking.

  • Keep the land public and protect it as a permanent municipal park.
  • Provide parking for residents of nearby buildings that lack adequate parking.
  • Serve Lower Lonsdale businesses, their customers and visitors.
  • Plan for autonomous vehicles to drive in, park, be stored and charge.

Keep the land public. Create the park. Put parking underground.
5.3

Protect strategic civic land

Public ownership must mean meaningful public control.

The default should be no sale of City land to private interests. Protect strategic civic properties for long-term public purposes, including against leases or other arrangements that surrender meaningful public control for generations.

Some City land is an asset to manage. Strategic civic land is an inheritance to protect.
5.4

Growth and infrastructure

Ask what today’s decision will mean in 20 or 30 years.

Link population growth to a long-term plan for roads, utilities, parks, recreation, transportation, public safety and community services.

  • Show construction cost.
  • Show operating and lifecycle cost.
  • Explain financing.
  • Include the value and opportunity cost of public land.
  • Show the effect on future taxpayers.
Can we build this now, and what will it mean in 20 or 30 years?
5.5

Provincial Housing Mandate Exemption

Housing objectives should recognize the physical reality of our community.

As Mayor, I would ask the Province to exempt the City of North Vancouver from the requirement to pre-zone sufficient land for the full provincial 20-year Housing Needs Report calculation. Growth planning should reflect existing density, limited land and the infrastructure needed to support it.

This would not mean stopping housing construction. We would continue planning for rental housing, family-sized housing, seniors housing, non-market housing and appropriate redevelopment.

This would not mean stopping housing construction.
5.6

Harry Jerome land and financing review

Review the financing before disposing of more public land.

Review whether the remaining Harry Jerome Neighbourhood Lands disposition strategy can lawfully and responsibly be reversed or restructured so the City can retain strategic public land. This concerns the land and financing, not undoing the completed recreation centre.

An executed lease or vested property right cannot simply be wished away. Any change must be based on the actual agreements, lawful rights, negotiated terms and full public cost.

5.7

Plan Moodyville as a neighbourhood

An OCP should plan a community, not just legalize the next project.

Bring a motion to Council for coordinated neighbourhood planning that considers The Trails and former TransLink depot OCP amendments in one community framework. Give residents meaningful choices before a preferred direction is fixed.

Explore concentrating development to create lasting public value: a substantial central green on The Trails, better family housing, useful shops and services, public amenities and connected walking routes.

If the City grants substantially more development capacity, what permanent improvement does the neighbourhood receive?
5.8

Coordinated utility renewal

When we open a street, plan the work together.

Renew infrastructure according to condition and risk. Coordinate water, sewer, gas, electricity and communications work before streets are excavated, and assess undergrounding overhead wires where the benefits justify the added cost.
