Policy Positions

Transportation

Everyone needs to be able to choose how they move around. Whether you walk, roll, drive or take transit, you should be able to do so safely and responsibly. And it should be fun too!

More

The City has built new protected mobility corridors, including Esplanade, Crosscut Bridge, and West 1st Street. These projects improve connections with our neighbours and help create a more complete transportation network. This work is not just about bikes. It is about freedom, independence, and accessibility, especially for people with disabilities, seniors, and anyone who uses mobility supports and needs safe, connected routes to move through the city.

There is more to do, and we need to continue working closely with the District of North Vancouver, TransLink, and our regional partners — but we are making real progress. Together with TransLink, we helped bring RapidBus service to the North Shore, supported by dedicated bus lanes that now move thousands of commuters more efficiently every day. The next step is Bus Rapid Transit: a faster, more reliable form of bus service with dedicated lanes, priority at intersections, frequent service, and better stations to move more people without requiring everyone to drive.

Experience here and around the world shows that we cannot simply build our way out of traffic congestion. The only real way to manage traffic is to give people better options. When more people can choose transit, walking, rolling, cycling, or other forms of mobility, our streets work better for everyone — including those who drive. These alternatives to single-occupancy vehicles are not just cheaper, cleaner, and more efficient; they also help make our city healthier, more accessible, and more fun.

Tony will:
  • Move public transit forward – Bus Rapid Transit creates bus priority to ensure people using transit are rewarded with time and advocate for a new Second Narrows crossing that enables public transit
  • Vote to create a safe and complete mobility network for all ages that supports transportation choices
  • Support active living by expanding shared transportation options and supporting improved lighting and sidewalks for pedestrians

Housing

By creating more housing options we can ensure more accessible and affordable housing options for all.

More

The City has made real progress in diversifying the shapes and sizes of homes available in our community. We have focused new housing near major corridors and frequent transit, including areas now supported by RapidBus service. This helps give more people the option to live close to work, school, services, and transportation.

Today, many new rental projects are coming online. Over the last eight years, the City has seen its largest increase in rental homes since the 1970s. I have supported stronger protections for long-time renters and worked to ensure people impacted by redevelopment receive support to relocate and remain connected to their community.

Looking ahead, I will continue to support more purpose-built rental housing, non-market housing, co-operative housing, co-housing, and workforce housing. We need homes for people across incomes — including seniors, youth, families, essential workers, and those most at risk of being pushed out.

We also need to better integrate rental housing with local commercial areas, including retail and office developments. The most vibrant and livable cities in the world combine homes, shops, services, public spaces, and transit so people can meet more of their daily needs close to home.

Housing is about more than buildings. It is about whether people can build a life here, stay connected to their community, and see a future for themselves in the City of North Vancouver.

Tony will:
  • Create more housing options in the right places by focusing new homes near frequent transit, services, shops, schools, and public spaces — while ensuring development contributes fairly to community amenities.
  • Support more affordable and non-market housing by expanding co-op, social, co-housing, workforce, and non-market options through federal and provincial funding, partnerships, and municipal tools.
  • Protect people through change by strengthening renter protections, supporting residents through redevelopment, and prioritizing long-time residents, seniors, youth, workers, and vulnerable households

Places for People

Managing and expanding municipal assets that ensure our quality of life is essential. The majority of our population lives in multi-family buildings without lots of outdoor spaces, so supporting public spaces is critical to enjoying our city and enhancing community connections.

More

When people live in apartments, condos, co-ops, and townhomes, shared public spaces become even more important. Parks, plazas, playgrounds, dog parks, street trees, sidewalks, mobility routes, sports courts, community facilities, and welcoming streets all become part of people’s everyday living space.

That is why I will continue to support investments in infrastructure and public spaces that serve the vast majority of our residents. Great public spaces help make compact living more livable, support mental and physical health, create opportunities for fitness and social connection, and strengthen the North Shore lifestyle we all value.

Public spaces also support local businesses and our local economy. When people have great places to gather, walk, shop, eat, play, and spend time, our commercial areas become more vibrant and resilient.

I have supported initiatives to expand public amenities across the city, including new parklets, more dog parks, expanded street tree coverage, improved active transportation connections, and new skate park facilities.

A more affordable city must also be a more livable city. That means building homes, but also investing in the public spaces and infrastructure that allow people to thrive.

Tony will:
  • Create greener, better-connected neighbourhoods by protecting mature trees, expanding street tree coverage, securing more green space through development, and supporting green mid-block pedestrian connectors, pocket parks, and improved access to transit.
  • Invest in accessible, people-focused public spaces with washrooms, seating, shade, trees, play areas, dog spaces, parklets, outdoor art, and gathering places that work for people of all ages and abilities.
  • Protect quality of life as the city grows by managing urban noise, addressing impacts from landscaping equipment, modified vehicle exhausts, and construction, while securing access to important recreation amenities like tennis and pickleball courts.
  • Expand small-scale public spaces across the city through parklets, barklets, dog runs, pocket parks, outdoor art, and better pedestrian connections that make dense neighbourhoods feel more livable, social, and green.

Jobs & the Economy

Championing small businesses, local entrepreneurs, and nationally significant employers, so our city remains vibrant, diverse, and prosperous.

More

A strong local economy means a stronger North Vancouver. When more people can work, shop, access services, and run businesses close to home, we reduce pressure on our transportation network and give people back more time with family, friends, and community.

Our economy is diverse and more significant than many people realize. Alongside our growing health-care sector, North Vancouver is home to major employers and nationally significant companies connected to the port, trade, marine, industrial, and export sectors. These businesses provide good local jobs, support regional supply chains, and play an important role in Canada’s economy — especially at a time when the country is under pressure to strengthen productivity, trade, infrastructure, and economic resilience.

Many small and family-run businesses are under real pressure. They are struggling to find workers, manage rising costs, and absorb high property taxes. This is where housing affordability, transportation, public space, and economic sustainability all come together. If people who work in North Vancouver cannot afford to live here — or cannot get here efficiently — local businesses, services, and institutions all feel the impact. That is why we need to advance workforce housing, improve transportation options, and continue investing in vibrant streets, plazas, patios, parklets, markets, events, and welcoming commercial areas that bring people together and support the foot traffic small businesses depend on.

The City’s updated 2024 Economic Strategy gives us a stronger framework to support this work, with a focus on strengthening employment lands, making it easier to do business, creating world-class public places, and building a resilient local economy. It also identifies opportunities to grow the health and innovation cluster around Lions Gate Hospital and revitalize Central Lonsdale as a destination for community and commercial activity. The creation of the Central Lonsdale Business Improvement Area is an important step, giving local businesses and property owners a stronger collective voice to support events, beautification, marketing, safety, and economic vitality in one of the City’s most important commercial districts.

Tony will:
  • Advocate for fairer business taxation so small businesses are not unfairly burdened by property taxes based on the “highest and best use” of land.
  • Build on our economic strengths by supporting health and innovation around Lions Gate Hospital, film and creative industries in Bridgman, and the port, marine, trade, and export sectors.
  • Advance workforce housing so more people who work in North Vancouver can afford to live here.
  • Support vibrant commercial districts through business improvement associations, better public spaces, patios, parklets, events, and welcoming streets.
  • Improve goods movement by exploring logistics hubs and practical solutions that reduce congestion while supporting local businesses.
