Housing

Our city should support diverse housing that allows young people, seniors, and families to stay in our community and share the same neighbourhoods. It’s not just how many units we need to build, it’s about who we are building them for. The people who live and work here should be able to choose North Vancouver as their home. This means mixed-use density in transit corridors, small-scale multi-unit gentle density in residential neighbourhoods, and increasing non-market housing and tenant protections.

Transportation

Our city should be easy and safe to get around for all our residents, businesses, and commuters. Our major transportation infrastructure isn’t municipal, but the city can help to develop solutions. Whatever our ride we’re all pedestrians with places to be, but we need good options to get us there. This means advocating for the expansion of Bus Rapid Transit, supporting an active transportation network, and providing those who already work here with more options to live here.

Parks & Public Spaces

Our city’s parks and public spaces should build community and promote wellbeing. They can provide social opportunities through recreation, gathering, and incidental encounters with neighbours. With increasing growth and density our parks and public spaces will be even more important. This means multi-functional, adaptive, and inclusive design. It is also an opportunity to engage local First Nations as partners to develop spaces that reflect the cultural heritage of the land.

Accountability

Our city should responsibly and transparently manage the funds that residents and businesses provide. If we can effectively promote civic literacy more people will engage with city hall proactively and positively. Council must manage difficult financial decisions with our long-term interest in mind. This means incorporating the new community engagement framework into the next council’s strategic plan, following through on the call for a public inquiry into the wastewater treatment plant, and monitoring debt servicing and the health of our reserves.

Resilience

Our city is increasingly vulnerable to climate change-related disruption and critical infrastructure failures. We all know what happens when one of our two crossings is compromised for hours, just imagine a week, or a month. Emergency preparedness requires redundancy to ensure our safety and security. This means mitigating climate change with more tree canopy and better drainage, reviewing our emergency management plans, and advocating for a redundant and seismically resilient crossing not for transportation, but for resilient critical infrastructure.

Biography

submitted by the candidate or their team
Jullian is an organizational culture and communications specialist, improvisor, and instructor. He ran for trustee in '18, '21, and '22, and is now running for council to represent underrepresented people and to help ensure that families that call the CNV home can continue to.

Hey, I’m Jullian Kolstee.

We might have crossed paths in North Vancouver over the years. I went to school at Westview, Carson Graham, and Capilano University, and played youth sports with NVFC (before it was NVFC) and Capilano RFC. You may have seen myself or my students perform with Carson Graham Improv, remember me from my student leadership at Carson or Capilano, or just seen me at the Delbrook pool a few times a week. Or maybe it was when I worked at Capilano Mall, the Laserdome, Presentation House, or one of the other local institutions I’ve been through. Whether we’ve met or not, I'm a 35 year old son of immigrants from Chile and the Netherlands and a neurodivergent person living with disabilities.

Public service has always been important to me, and my family. As a child my father was brain injured and my mother struggled to provide for my brother and I working odd jobs and stretching his teacher's pension. Were it not for labour unions, non-profits, public education, public health, and the volunteerism and generosity of this community, my family and I would not be here. We lost my father to COVID-19 in December of 2020. My father’s legacy as an educator in our community, particularly with indigenous peoples, set an example for me to follow throughout my life.

Professionally, I’ve spent the past seven years with an international medical device manufacturer working to lead their organizational culture strategy and trying to improve the working lives of hundreds of workers. I also have more than 20 years of experience performing and teaching improvisational theatre, developing skills that allow me to communicate and connect without being cold or polished.

Politically I’ve been active in the community since my youth and served three years elected to the Capilano Students Union. I was a candidate in the past two municipal elections for school board as an independent with no resources. I received an endorsement from the NVTA and increased my vote totals in those campaigns. I have supported political campaigns before, have liaised with MPs and MLAs, and have relationships with local leaders on the North Shore.

My mother Maribell, my partner Sinéad, and I all continue to make our home here in the City of North Vancouver, despite the fact that we struggle to afford to live here. My family came to North Vancouver because of my father Anton’s work at Carson Graham Secondary. When he suffered a heart attack and became brain injured, the community rallied behind us by holding a benefit event, while friends and colleagues reached out and offered their personal assistance. I could list all the subsidies and bursaries I have received, or the countless volunteers who made an impact on me, but the fact is I can never repay my debt to my community. I hope you will all support my campaign to serve as councillor for the City of North Vancouver.

Reason for running

submitted by the candidate or their team
When my family fell on hard times this community supported us. I’ve been the beneficiary of your kindness and generosity throughout my life. North Vancouver has invested in me, and I’m here to return that investment on council.

I’ve grown up and lived in North Vancouver all my life, and I’ve witnessed how this community has transformed. That change is only accelerating, and I believe our challenge is to grow while protecting what makes this place our home.

When my family fell on hard times, this community supported us. I’ve been the beneficiary of your kindness and generosity throughout my life, and I’ve tried to serve through my civic engagement, teaching, volunteering, advocacy, and public service. North Vancouver has invested in me, and I’m here to return that investment on council.

I’m running for City Council because I want to bring my experiences and skills to serving North Vancouver. I want to serve our long-term public interest as a councillor who approaches the issues that affect us most by engaging in difficult conversations, building real relationships, and drawing from diverse perspectives.

Politicians often talk about young people being priced out of the communities they belong to. I am that person. I am not a talking point. I’m running for City Council because as a son of North Vancouver, I want a future here.
