Candidate Responses 2026 Municipal Election
Questions
1. If elected, what is your vision for improved transportation in North Vancouver?
2. Costs are rising and small business is feeling the squeeze. What will you do to
support small businesses and foster business and job growth in North Vancouver?
City of North Vancouver
Councillor
Don Bell
1. Traffic congestion is a problem affecting movement of people and commercial
traffic in North Van. We have road allowances created over 100 years ago with
smaller populations. Improvements can be made to public transit, existing roads
and signalization. Plan for increased growth of commercial truck delivery of goods
and services to residents, local businesses and industry.
We also must meet future needs for movement of employees, retail customers,
medical visits, restaurant workers and customers, and commuters both to and from
our community. Future options include making public transit more frequent,
dependable , affordable and convenient, hopefully encouraging commuters who
don’t need a car for their work to take transit.
Include both vehicle and transit capacity in future improvements of the Iron Workers
Memorial Bridge and possibly a North Shore version of Skytrain or new fast rail
commuter service. The recently announced R2 Rapid Bus Park Royal to Metrotown
is a step in the right direction. Providing truly affordable housing options would
allow more business employees to live closer to their employment.
The major options for improving transportation will require significant funding and
co-operation from senior levels of government. North Van residents and businesses
must be included in planning future transportation.
2. Small and “Mom & Pop” retail businesses, medical, professional and technical
offices, restaurants, pubs and coffee shops bring vitality of our local economy.
Starting with the Covid Pandemic followed by economic uncertainty, both world-
wide and locally, are challenges businesses have faced.
Affordable housing options are needed for local workers to be able to live closer to
their work and reduce the need for long distance commuting, especially if it involves
crossing bridges to the North Shore. Public transit that is more frequent,
dependable , affordable and convenient will help businesses attract and retain
employees.
Streamline City licencing and permit processes, and approval times to reduce costs
that businesses require starting up or renovating. Enhancing the street level
shopping experience by street trees, landscaping and street benches that allow
shoppers to rest.
Improvements to street customer parking, including better signage identifying
nearby off-street public parking would help attract customers. Review with
businesses possible improvements to the Pay Parking program, including the
amount of free parking time.
Review the residential to business tax ratios of the annual tax allocation compared
to other similar communities. Protect our existing Light Industrial areas and their
value to our economy.
