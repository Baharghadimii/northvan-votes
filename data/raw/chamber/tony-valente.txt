Candidate Responses 2026 Municipal Election
Questions
1. If elected, what is your vision for improved transportation in North Vancouver?
2. Costs are rising and small business is feeling the squeeze. What will you do to
support small businesses and foster business and job growth in North Vancouver?
City of North Vancouver
Councillor
Tony Valente
1. My vision for improved transportation is focused on one question: how do we move
more people, more efficiently, through the limited road space we have?
That starts with delivering the North Shore’s first major rapid transit option. If re-
elected, I will continue to champion Bus Rapid Transit, with dedicated lanes, transit
priority, frequent service and well-designed stations. BRT can provide a fast, reliable
alternative to driving while making better use of the corridors we already have.
We should also use new technology and smarter transportation systems to improve
traffic flow, manage congestion and make transit more reliable. Longer term, I will
continue advocating with regional and provincial partners for a new Second
Narrows crossing.
My approach is also about choice. Whether you walk, roll, cycle, drive or take
transit, you should be able to move safely and efficiently. Before being elected, I
chaired the HUB North Shore Committee, and on Council I have supported the
City’s Mobility Strategy, protected mobility routes, RapidBus and expanded shared
micromobility.
Active transportation remains important, especially for shorter trips and connecting
people to transit. The larger goal is a system that moves more people, not simply
more vehicles.
2. Small businesses are the backbone of North Vancouver’s economy, and if re-
elected, I will continue supporting the conditions they need to succeed.
A strong local economy depends on more than business policy alone. We need
housing choices, transportation options, public spaces and amenities that allow
people to live, work and spend time in our community. More housing helps
employers attract and retain talent, allows more workers to live closer to their jobs,
and brings customers within walking distance of local shops and services.
Great public spaces are also economic infrastructure. The Shipyards has shown
how thoughtful investment in the public realm can draw people into an area,
increase foot traffic and create opportunities for restaurants, retailers and other
businesses. As a founding member and director of the North Van Urban Forum, I
helped bring residents together around the future of the waterfront and Shipyards
district.
I will also continue advocating for fairer property taxation, including for businesses
affected by “highest and best use” assessments, support strong BIAs, and
encourage commercial spaces in new developments that work for local businesses.
