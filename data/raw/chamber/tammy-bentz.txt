Candidate Responses 2026 Municipal Election
Questions
1. If elected, what is your vision for improved transportation in North Vancouver?
2. Costs are rising and small business is feeling the squeeze. What will you do to
support small businesses and foster business and job growth in North Vancouver?
City of North Vancouver
Councillor
Tammy Bentz
1. North Vancouver doesn’t need another transportation plan that looks beautiful in a
binder while everyone is stuck in traffic. We need a transportation system that
actually works.
My vision is simple: make it easier to get where you’re going—whether you’re
walking, rolling, biking, taking the bus, or driving. That means safer, connected
walking and cycling routes, better transit frequency and reliability, and serious
advocacy for rapid transit across the North Shore.
But being pro-transit doesn’t mean being anti-car. People still need vehicles—
especially tradespeople, caregivers, families, people with disabilities and
businesses moving goods. We should stop pretending that every transportation
problem can be solved by adding another bike lane, or another lane for cars.
I’d push for practical, data-driven decisions about how we use our limited street
space, with priority for safety, transit, emergency response and essential deliveries.
And I’d measure whether projects actually improve travel times and safety—not just
whether we successfully spent the money.
The goal? A North Vancouver where getting around is less of a daily endurance
sport.
2. Small businesses don’t need City Hall to “support” them by giving them another 47-
page form to fill out. They need us to make it easier to open, operate and grow a
business.
I’d focus on three things: less bureaucracy, more affordability and better streets. The
City’s own Economic Strategy calls for simpler licensing, coordinated services and
better understanding of the cost pressures facing small businesses. I’d make those
commitments real, measurable and accountable.
That means a genuine one-stop business service at City Hall, faster and more
predictable approvals, regular conversations with business owners, and a serious
review of municipal fees, regulations and processes that add costs without adding
value.
We also need to make our commercial neighbourhoods places people want to
visit—not merely places they drive through. Good public spaces, sensible parking
and curb management, attractive main streets and support for local entrepreneurs
can help create the foot traffic that small businesses depend on.
And I want North Vancouver attracting good jobs and innovative businesses—not
just more condos with a coffee shop at ground level.
Economic development should mean more opportunity, not more paperwork.
