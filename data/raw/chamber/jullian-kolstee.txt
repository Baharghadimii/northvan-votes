Candidate Responses 2026 Municipal Election
Questions
1. If elected, what is your vision for improved transportation in North Vancouver?
2. Costs are rising and small business is feeling the squeeze. What will you do to
support small businesses and foster business and job growth in North Vancouver?
City of North Vancouver
Councillor
Jullian Kolstee
1. Transportation should be safe, reliable, and accessible for residents and
businesses, whatever the mode. Our major infrastructure and public transit are
inadequate, but the city can only advocate along with the districts of North and
West Vancouver to the province and Translink respectively. Bus Rapid Transit is what
is available to us for now, and that requires transit priority in order to bypass
congestion. Improving our active transportation network and streetscapes are tools
that the city has to incentivize alternative modes of transportation, along with
denser mixed-use communities that commuters can afford to live in.
I’m most concerned with the resilience of our transportation infrastructure. We’re
well aware of how disruptive a simple accident on one crossing can be, and that
would be multiplied were a crossing to be compromised, or in the event of a major
emergency on the North Shore. Escalating the need for resilient and redundant
critical infrastructure from a transportation issue to an emergency management
issue shifts the priority ahead, and I believe that is appropriate in our case.
2. Our city should partner with small businesses as stakeholders in our community
and local economy. These local businesses offer employment to residents, provide
us with services, and are essential contributors to the city’s revenues. This means
offering businesses clear processes to start and operate in the city, ensuring that
streets and public spaces are attractive to customers, and that the city’s services
meet their needs. Working with the city should be predictable for small businesses,
easy to understand and navigate.
The city’s economic development is tied to transportation infrastructure for goods,
services, and labour, as well as affordability for workers to be able to live where they
work, and for local patrons of small businesses. One metric I would use to judge our
success is the longevity of businesses in the city. As a councillor I am looking for
long-term partners in the business community who want to contribute to the future
of our city.
