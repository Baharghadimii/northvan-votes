Candidate Responses 2026 Municipal Election
Questions
1. If elected, what is your vision for improved transportation in North Vancouver?
2. Costs are rising and small business is feeling the squeeze. What will you do to
support small businesses and foster business and job growth in North Vancouver?
City of North Vancouver
Councillor
Anna Boltenko
1. My vision is a North Vancouver where people, workers and goods can move
reliably—not a city where everyone loses hours to congestion.
Transportation is an economic issue. I will press for faster, more frequent and
dependable transit; practical bus-priority measures; and sustained advocacy to
TransLink and the Province for rapid transit connecting the North Shore to the
regional network. The City must coordinate closely with the District, Squamish
Nation and regional partners because congestion does not stop at municipal
boundaries.
Within the City, I will support safer, connected walking and cycling routes,
accessible sidewalks, better links to transit and safer school zones. These choices
give more people a realistic alternative to driving and free road capacity for trades,
deliveries and those who must travel by car.
Businesses also need dependable loading access, sensible curb management and
better coordination of construction and roadwork. Finally, housing near jobs,
services and transit must be part of the transportation solution. My approach is not
cars versus bikes or buses. It is about moving more people efficiently, safely and
predictably.
2. As a nonprofit founder and active Chamber member, I understand that “support
local” must mean more than a slogan. Small businesses need City Hall to be
responsive, predictable and easy to navigate.
I will push for a one-stop business navigator, clear service standards and published
timelines for licences, permits and inspections. The City should review fees and
processes with small-business owners, eliminate duplication and communicate
changes before they create unexpected costs.
We must protect employment lands and encourage flexible, appropriately sized
commercial spaces so independent businesses, startups and service providers can
remain here. City procurement should give local and Indigenous small businesses a
fair opportunity to compete.
Business growth also depends on workforce stability. More attainable housing,
childcare and reliable transportation will help employers recruit and retain people.
Vibrant streets, festivals, markets, patios and welcoming public spaces bring
customers to local businesses.
I will work with the Chamber, BIAs and entrepreneurs to implement the City’s
Economic Strategy—with measurable targets, regular reporting and a simple test: is
it becoming easier to start, operate and grow a business in North Vancouver?
