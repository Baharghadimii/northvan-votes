Candidate Responses 2026 Municipal Election
Questions
1. If elected, what is your vision for improved transportation in North Vancouver?
2. Costs are rising and small business is feeling the squeeze. What will you do to
support small businesses and foster business and job growth in North Vancouver?
City of North Vancouver
Mayor
Dave Kurvers
1. North Vancouver needs transportation that fits North Vancouver. I do not think the
answer is simply more buses on already congested streets.
Regional rapid transit and local North Shore movement are different problems.
Regional rapid transit can terminate in North Vancouver, with passengers
transferring into a local east-west network designed around North Shore
destinations. That network could include privately developed urban ropeways
providing frequent, grade-separated service.
We also need to get our priorities right at street level. The City’s pre-pandemic 2019
North Shore Transportation Survey found that 19.6% of daily trips by City residents
were on foot and 2.1% by bicycle. The sidewalk is our busiest active-transportation
network and, on a commercial street, it is the critical connection between
storefronts and customers.
A bike lane is not automatically an improvement if it breaks that relationship or
removes needed loading, deliveries, trades access and short-term customer
access. Before changing a commercial street, the City should measure those
impacts before and after.
My goal is reliable local movement, strong regional connections and transportation
infrastructure that supports neighbourhood life and local business.
2. My approach is business retention before business attraction. Before chasing the
next investment, City Hall should make sure the businesses already here can
survive, expand and reinvest.
A complete, straightforward business-licence application should receive a City
decision within one week. Commercial permits should have published service
standards, clear checklists, early zoning confirmation and one City contact
coordinating the file. Where a new business takes over substantially the same lawful
use, we should carry forward valid approvals and infrastructure wherever legally
possible instead of starting from zero.
City delay also has a real cost. Businesses can burn rent, wages and working capital
while waiting for approvals. I would track City-controlled delay, repeat reviews and
whether service standards are actually being met.
I also want an annual Local Business Health Report, with quarterly permitting
metrics, so Council can see openings, closures, vacancies and whether City
decisions are helping or hurting commercial streets.
Before changing parking, loading, vehicle access, curb space or streetscape design,
the City should assess the business impact and measure the results afterward.
A City that says it is open for business should be able to prove it.
