Candidate Responses 2026 Municipal Election
Questions
1. If elected, what is your vision for improved transportation in North Vancouver?
2. Costs are rising and small business is feeling the squeeze. What will you do to
support small businesses and foster business and job growth in North Vancouver?
District of North Vancouver
Councillor
Trey Bell
1. I support an all-of-the-above approach to transportation: practical road
improvements, better public transit, and safer, more connected options for walking
and cycling. We cannot solve congestion by focusing on only one way of getting
around.
I would support having two southbound lanes on Mountain Highway approaching
the highway to alleviate the Arborlynn bottleneck, expand community bus service,
and work with TransLink and senior governments to improve transit and advance
rapid transit, such as SkyTrain to North Vancouver, starting with connections to
Phibbs Exchange and Capilano University. The District’s current financial plan
already identifies rapid transit and improvements to the Highway 1 corridor and
Second Narrows crossing as priorities requiring regional and senior-government
partnerships.
I also want to complete missing sidewalk and cycling connections, particularly in
growing neighbourhoods where infrastructure has not kept pace with development.
Most importantly, I believe future growth must be accompanied by the
transportation infrastructure needed to support it. My goal if elected would be to
provide residents more transportation choices while reducing congestion, and
making it easier and safer to get around North Vancouver.
2. Small businesses are vital to our local economy, and the District should make it
easier to operate and grow a business in North Vancouver. Through my time working
at Bee Haven Childcare, from a centre manager to a program director, I saw
firsthand how a well run small business can grow, meet the needs of the
community, employ local people, and create value far beyond its walls.
I would work to streamline municipal permits and approvals, reduce unnecessary
red tape, keep municipal taxes and fees reasonable, and engage directly with
businesses before making decisions that affect them. I will not support introducing
paid street parking in the District of North Vancouver. Access matters for customers,
employees and local businesses.
I also want to support vibrant town centres where residents can shop, work and
access everyday services close to home. Future planning should ensure growing
neighbourhoods have the commercial spaces, shops and services needed to
become genuinely walkable communities.
Transportation and housing are also economic issues. We should work with local
employers to improve transportation connections and encourage diverse housing
options so people who work in North Vancouver have a better opportunity to live
here too.
