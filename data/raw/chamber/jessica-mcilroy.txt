Candidate Responses 2026 Municipal Election
Questions
1. If elected, what is your vision for improved transportation in North Vancouver?
2. Costs are rising and small business is feeling the squeeze. What will you do to
support small businesses and foster business and job growth in North Vancouver?
City of North Vancouver
Councillor
Jessica McIlroy
1. It’s important for the city to address mobility in two separate ways, the movement of
people and goods within the city, and the movement of people and goods on and off
the North Shore. The existing mobility strategy has created actions that address safe
and accessible transportation options for residents within the city, prioritizing active
transportation, transit, and mode share. We need to continue implementing these
actions to support a greater diversity of mobility options, improve pedestrian and
cyclist safety, and decrease emissions while improving health outcomes.
The major transportation issues and frustrations faced by North Vancouver
residents relate mostly to movement on and off the North Shore. High traffic
volumes and accidents cause bridge delays, leading to rat running through
residential neighbourhoods and inefficient movement in the region. To address this
larger issue, all North Shore governments must work together to ensure bus rapid
transit is implemented while advocating to the province for a plan to replace the
Ironworkers Memorial Bridge.
2. The most important things within the city’s control to support local businesses are
to streamline and simplify permitting processes, find ways to ease tax burdens such
as through a development potential relief program or permitted tax exemptions,
invest in regional improvements through plans such as the Great Street Project. The
city can also create policies that define retail and commercial unit sizes in new
buildings, to encourage smaller units for local retail and support medical and
industrial spaces where needed. A more defined policy is needed on what type of
businesses are preferred for streetscape spaces vs off-street and second or third
floor spaces.
The city can also advocate to the province for changes to property assessments,
triple net leases, and to fund and reopen Small Business BC. There has been a long
standing challenge with the assessment process using “highest and best use”
calculations, which affects property taxes and how those costs are passed on to
tenants. Given that over 90% of businesses in BC are small businesses, an
organization that provides support services is needed.
