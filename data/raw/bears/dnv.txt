Co-existing with North Shore Bears: Candidate Responses
District of North Vancouver
2026 municipal election. Compiled by North Shore Bear Advocates. Responses received September 29 to October 2, 2026.
The question we asked:
The following email was sent to every candidate for Mayor and Council on September 29, 2026:


Thank you for putting your name forward in this election. I'm writing to ask about an issue that matters to many North Shore residents: how we live alongside our black bears.


Bears are a huge asset to the North Shore. They are part of what makes this place special, and they reflect the health of the forests and creeks we all enjoy. This year we are seeing more bear activity, and bears are turning up further into our neighbourhoods than before. The result has been heartbreaking: many bears have been killed, often because they became used to human food and garbage. In almost every case, the bear pays the price for a problem people created.
Education is important, but it hasn't been enough to prevent these deaths. So I'd like to ask you one question:


Besides education, what specific mechanism would you implement in your municipality to better co-exist with bears?


I'm interested in concrete steps.


I'd be grateful for your response by Friday, October 2nd. I plan to share candidates' responses with the broader community, and will note any candidates who don't reply.


Thank you for your time. I look forward to hearing your ideas.


~L
Lindsey Turner
North Shore Bear Advocates

Candidates for Mayor
Stephan Nieweler
Received October 2, 2026
The death of bears because of interactions with human activities is a significant problem in the District of North Vancouver. Current policies and protocols must be revisited based on best practice in other jurisdictions with similar issues.
Education is an important tool for reducing human-bear conflict, as new and some existing residents may not have dealt with wildlife issues in other cities or other parts of Metro Vancouver, prior to their move to North Vancouver. The forms of education utilized should be reviewed, however, in order to maximize their effectiveness and target a broader cross-section of District residents.
Aside from education, we must focus on household, commercial and municipal waste infrastructure, as well as regulations pertaining to the time waste is located at the curbside. It would be beneficial for the municipality to facilitate the supply of bear resistant bins, potentially reducing the cost for users through bulk purchase. The time that these bins are located at the roadside on collection day should also be reviewed, in order to minimize the opportunity for bears to interact with waste. The applicability and level of fines should be reviewed in order to increase compliance.
We should also take planning and maintenance measures to reduce the opportunity for bears to enter human occupied areas, including the strategic use of bear resistant fencing at the urban-nature interface, buffer zones where berries bush growth is minimized, the elimination of urban sprawl and the acquisition of strategic properties to enhance the connectivity of natural areas.

Trey Bell
Received September 29, 2026
Hi Lindsey,
Thank you for reaching out. I am Dismayed by the number of bears being killed in our community. Beyond education, one of my biggest priorities is protecting the habitat that bears and other wildlife depend on. I am one of the few candidates in the DNV municipal election, who has been consistently speaking up for stronger protection of our forests and against continued urban sprawl. I believe this is directly connected to keeping bears safer on the North Shore. When forests and large stands of mature trees are removed for development, we continue reducing and fragmenting wildlife habitat and increasing interactions between bears and people.
If elected, I would advocate for stronger protection of our remaining forests and natural areas and oppose further urban sprawl into these areas. I also believe we need to listen to the organizations that have spent years working on bear coexistence. I have met with the North Shore Black Bear Society (NSBBS) as a candidate, and I also have a personal connection to the organization, as my father Kevin Bell was formerly co-chair of the NSBBS Board of Directors.
From my meeting with the North Shore Black Bear Society, I learned that the District needs to take more concrete action around waste management in townhouse strata communities, where human-bear conflicts have been increasing. This is an area where the DNV can be more proactive by working with strata communities to improve waste storage and management practices before conflicts occur and utilize existing bylaws if strata’s aren’t taking appropriate actions.
I would engage directly with the North Shore Black Bear Society on the specific municipal measures they believe would be most effective, and work to implement practical recommendations within the District’s jurisdiction. I would also advocate for the creation of a North Shore-wide committee that brings together representatives from all three municipal councils, organizations such as the North Shore Black Bear Society, and the Provincial Government. Bears do not recognize municipal boundaries, so protecting them requires a coordinated approach that gets everyone working together toward consistent policies and practical solutions across the North Shore. My priorities would be keeping forests standing, protecting wildlife habitat, reducing preventable human-bear conflicts, and doing everything we reasonably can to keep both bears and people safe. Thanks again for your question and I hope we can elect municipal leaders that take this issue seriously across the North Shore.

Betty Forbes
Received October 2, 2026
Hi Lindsey,
I am running for my third term as Councillor for the District of North Vancouver.
It was heartwarming to read your letter about co-existing with North Shore Bears. Education is very important but to protect the bears/cubs more has to be done. You are absolutely correct about the many causes of the increased larger numbers of bears breaching our urban containment boundary, but it comes down to food, especially before hibernation. There have been so many mother bears killed leaving their cubs to die from starvation, killed by other bears, & wildlife as they are too young to have learnt all their life lessons from mom. Cubs usually stay with their mom until 18 months old. The COS either kills them, leaves them wherever they are found, and even when a wildlife sanctuary with room to take the cubs offers to take them, the COS often withholds permission to let them go to the help offered. The cubs perish.
Two years ago I submitted a motion to my council requesting changes (5) that I wanted to get passed for more humane treatment of these sentient beings. It passed. The resolution was then presented to the Lower Mainland Local Government Association (LMLGA) and was passed. This meant that the motion would go forward as a resolution to the Union of BC Municipalities (UBCM) annual convention. At this convention I attended a presentation by Conservation Officer Services (COS)(this is the Provincial body who makes the decision on how bears are treated, abandoned, killed. I then went to where all the municipality members go to hear the resolutions being presented and the sponsering municipality speaks in favor of their resolution (me on behalf of District of North Vancouver) and then the floor is open to everyone who wishes to speak (For / Against). Then the vote was called ... and it didn't pass.
Had it passed, UBCM would have adopted it and sent it to the Provincial Ministry who oversees the COS for review, implementation. I had worked on this for a year and was heartbroken.
I have connected with many people who are experts about bears. I have also connected with Councillors in other municipalities. Recently I connected with a Councillor and we have both agreed to work towards a Sea-to-Sky (North Van to Whistler) safer corridor for the bears. Part of this will probably mean trying to get a UBCM resolution passed at its next convention in 2027..
My more immediate actions if I am re-elected would be to increase the fines and enforcement, look into a better system and/or better bear "proof" garbage cans, and restart the free fruit picking for anyone who is unable to do this.
Yours, in community and conversation.

Kulvir Mann
Received October 2, 2026
Thank you for your email and question: Besides education, what specific mechanisms would you implement in your municipality to better co-exist with bears?
I have been collaborating with North Shore Black Bear Society to share out education materials to students at schools and parents in the community through my work with North Shore Safety Council for over 10 years.
To move beyond education and create urban co-existance with bears, municilipalities need to enforce the following:
infrastructure upgrades, targeted regulatory penalties and planning solutions.
* Automated Lock-and-Latch Carts - these are the carts everyone has - but residents need to be diligent that there is no damage and they are latched correctly
* Enclosed Commercial Enclosures - small businesses, multi family complexes and parks to house the dumpsters in mandatory heavy duty concrete and steel bear resistant enclosures
* Stricter Curbside Timing and Enforced by Bylaws - for those not following rules to timing of putting out garbage
* Ban High- Attractant Vegetation - update municipal landscaping guidelines to prohibit planting high risk attractants
* Create wildlife corridors - conduct a bear hazard assessment and apply zoning laws to steer bears around residential hubs instead of through them
* Community Fruit Gleaning Network - we already have a lot of volunteers doing this - increase the call out for this and get more people engaged and take part
Education is always important so I would recommend the "Bear Smart Community Program" resources and the key components they have compiled:
Key Components
1. Establish a Bear Smart Committee
Oversees planning and implementation.
Includes local government, wildlife agencies, NGOs, and community members.
2. Conduct a Community Assessment
Identifies attractants (garbage, fruit trees, livestock feed).
Reviews local bear activity and conflict history
3. Develop a Bear Smart Community Plan
Outlines strategies for waste management, education, enforcement, and habitat considerations.
4. Implement the Plan
Install bear‑resistant bins.
Improve bylaws and enforcement.
Conduct public education campaigns.
A brochure outlining the program and a technical background report are available:
Be 'Bear Smart' Community Brochure (PDF, 7MB)
Bear Smart Community Program: Background Report (PDF, 527KB)
I think this is a big issue and we all have to do our part to work collaboratively together and protect the bears and prevent and safety issues that could occur.
Best,
Kulvir

Victoria Nwabuisi
Received October 2, 2026
Hello Lindsey,
Thank you for your email.
It is heartbreaking to witness bears being killed in our community. We have moved into their habitat and, as the species with greater power and control, we often expect them to move out. Unfortunately, that is not how coexistence works.
I don’t think dividing ourselves into two camps—the “kill the bears” camp and the “don’t leave attractants out” camp—gets us closer to a solution. It encourages finger-pointing and judgement rather than collaboration.
Ultimately, I believe we can agree on something fundamental: we want people to feel safe, and we want to find a way to live alongside the wildlife that makes North Vancouver such a special place. That is where I would start.
Two concrete steps I would propose are:
1. Strengthen waste management during peak bear season.
Explore prohibiting curbside garbage collection during peak bear season and require residents and commercial properties to use secure, bear-resistant communal waste infrastructure or heavy-duty locking bear bins.
2. Create a community wildlife forum.
Bring together wildlife experts, conservation organizations, residents and community stakeholders for a public forum focused on practical coexistence. The goal would be to better understand what is happening, hear community concerns, examine what is and isn’t working, and identify solutions we can realistically implement.
We cannot undo the fact that we share this landscape with bears. But we can make better choices about how we live alongside them.
Thank you again for the opportunity to share my thoughts.
Yours truly,
Victoria Nwabuisi

Hi Lindsey and thank you for all the work you do to protect bears on the North Shore. Here is my response to your question and thank you for the opportunity.
Besides education, I would focus on preventing bears from being rewarded with human food.
That means enforcing existing attractant bylaws, particularly around garbage and organics, which are the biggest sources of reported bear attractants on the North Shore. I would also work with bear advocates to identify problem areas and focus municipal resources where they are most needed.
We should also make it easier for residents to do the right thing, including ensuring municipal facilities and public spaces have appropriate systems for securing garbage and other attractants.
Bears don't recognize municipal boundaries, so continued coordination between the three North Shore municipalities, the Bear Society and the Conservation Officer Service is essential. The goal is simple: prevent conflicts before they happen and keep both bears and residents safe.
