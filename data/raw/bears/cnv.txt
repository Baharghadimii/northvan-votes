Co-existing with North Shore Bears: Candidate Responses
City of North Vancouver
2026 municipal election. Compiled by North Shore Bear Advocates. Responses received September 29 to October 2, 2026.
The question we asked
The following email was sent to every candidate for Mayor and Council on September 29, 2026:


Thank you for putting your name forward in this election. I'm writing to ask about an issue that matters to many North Shore residents: how we live alongside our black bears.


Bears are a huge asset to the North Shore. They are part of what makes this place special, and they reflect the health of the forests and creeks we all enjoy. This year we are seeing more bear activity, and bears are turning up further into our neighbourhoods than before. The result has been heartbreaking: many bears have been killed, often because they became used to human food and garbage. In almost every case, the bear pays the price for a problem people created.
Education is important, but it hasn't been enough to prevent these deaths. So I'd like to ask you one question:


Besides education, what specific mechanism would you implement in your municipality to better co-exist with bears?


I'm interested in concrete steps.


I'd be grateful for your response by Friday, October 2nd. I plan to share candidates' responses with the broader community, and will note any candidates who don't reply.


Thank you for your time. I look forward to hearing your ideas.


~L
Lindsey Turner
North Shore Bear Advocates

Mike McGraw
Received October 2, 2026
Hi Lindsey,
Thanks for asking this, and for the reminder. Three bears killed in one week tells us education alone isn't working, and in almost every case the cause is something people can control.
The City already has some of the right rules. Our solid waste bylaw says carts can't go out before 5:30 a.m. on collection day and that containers must keep animals out, with fines up to $2,000. But those rules don't mention wildlife, and they don't cover fruit trees, bird feeders or other attractants. Here's what I'd push for:
A wildlife attractant bylaw that covers garbage, green bins, fallen fruit, bird feeders and unsecured dumpsters, with bylaw officers actively enforcing the set-out rules we already have, starting in neighbourhoods near ravines and greenbelts where bears come into the City.
Bear-resistant containers as the standard for City public bins along greenbelts and trails, and for multi-family and commercial buildings, with a reasonable phase-in. As a strata president, I believe most buildings will do it if the rule is clear.
A partnership with volunteer gleaning groups so residents can get help picking fruit they can't use before it drops.
One North Shore approach. Bears move between all three municipalities, and the District has already done the work to be recognized as a Bear Smart Community. The City should align its rules with the District and West Vancouver, share sighting and conflict data with the Conservation Officer Service and WildSafeBC, and report publicly on whether conflicts are going down.
Thanks,
Mike McGraw
Candidate for Mayor, City of North Vancouver

Anna Boltenko
Received September 29, 2026
Dear Lindsey,
Thank you for asking this important question. I was heartbroken by the killing of the bear seen throughout Lower Lonsdale today. Bears are not intruders in our community—we share this place with them. We have a responsibility to prevent human-created attractants from turning their presence in our neighbourhoods into a death sentence.
Beyond education, my concrete commitment would be to strengthen the City’s waste system through a comprehensive Bear-Smart and Wildlife Attractant Action Plan. I would ask Council to:
* Phase in certified bear-resistant garbage and organics containers, beginning near ravines, creeks and known wildlife corridors, while upgrading public bins and requiring secure dumpsters for multi-family and commercial properties.
* Provide rebates or a container-loan program so seniors, lower-income households, renters, stratas and non-profits can comply without carrying an unfair cost.
* Strengthen proactive bylaw enforcement while helping people comply: practical assistance and warnings first, followed by meaningful penalties for repeated violations. The City should also provide clear, multilingual guidance for residents, stratas, property managers and businesses on securing waste, responding safely to bear sightings and protecting both people and wildlife.
* Complete a Bear Hazard Assessment and establish a coordinated response protocol with the other North Shore municipalities, the Sḵwx̱wú7mesh Nation, the North Shore Black Bear Society and the Conservation Officer Service. This should include rapid neighbourhood alerts, attractant inspections, shared data and non-lethal intervention wherever public safety permits.
The City already has morning collection rules and secure-storage requirements. The missing piece is ensuring that every household and business has the proper equipment, information and support—and that repeated non-compliance has consequences.
Nearby communities, including Squamish, Lions Bay, Port Moody, Coquitlam and Whistler, have demonstrated that education must be backed by bear-resistant waste infrastructure, enforceable attractant bylaws, neighbourhood monitoring and a coordinated response. North Vancouver should learn from these models and adapt the strongest measures to our more urban setting.
The municipality cannot control every decision made by provincial wildlife authorities. But it can act much earlier, before a bear becomes food-conditioned and the available options narrow dramatically. We should measure success by fewer dangerous encounters, safer residents and fewer bears killed.
I believe we can—and must—do better.
Warmly,
Anna Boltenko

Hello Lindsy,
Thank you for the opportunity to provide respond to your question as a councillor candidate for the City of North Vancouver.
In response to your question, most of the North Shore's bear conflict is in the Districts of North Vancouver and West Vancouver, but bears do come down our ravines into the City. When they find garbage behind a townhouse complex, regrettably it is usually the bear that pays for it. Here are three actions I would suggest the City take:
1) Give City bylaws real force - the City already requires garbage and green cans to be stored where wildlife can't reach them. I would add a morning-only set-out window similar to the District, and match District fines. The bylaw should be consistent across municipal boundaries when a bear crosses it.
2) From my career in strata management, shared bins at townhouses and multi-family buildings are where this problem concentrates. As we add homes, wildlife-secure waste rooms should be a condition of approval. Existing strata/rental buildings next to ravines should possibly receive City support to retrofit.
3) There should be focus on ravine edge - fruit gleaning on City land, fencing standards for backyard hens and hives, and bear-resistant bins in parks along the ravine greenways.
A fourth, long-term goal, would be for the City to pursue Bear Smart status, as the District has, and publish the results every year.
Thank you again for the opportunity to respond to this important issue.
Sincerely,
Angela Girard
